How to Choose a Cordless Drill

A cordless drill is a useful tool for furniture assembly, home maintenance, and DIY projects. Choosing the right one depends on the materials you work with, how often you use it, and the battery system you prefer.

1. Understand Drill Types

A drill-driver is designed for drilling holes and driving screws. Hammer drills add a hammering action for certain masonry tasks. Impact drivers are separate tools primarily designed for driving fasteners.

2. Compare Battery Systems

Cordless tools commonly use rechargeable lithium-ion batteries. Consider the manufacturer's battery platform, available tools, charger compatibility, and replacement battery cost. Voltage alone does not determine overall performance.

3. Consider Power and Speed

Adjustable speed and torque settings help with different materials and fasteners. A clutch can reduce the chance of overdriving screws. Compare performance using reliable specifications and independent testing where available.

4. Check Size and Comfort

A compact drill may be easier to handle in tight spaces. Consider weight with the battery installed, grip comfort, balance, and how long you expect to use it.

5. Look at Useful Features

Features worth considering include keyless chucks, work lights, variable-speed triggers, brushless motors, and included carrying cases. Decide which features you will actually use.

6. Compare Kits and Bare Tools

Some drills are sold with batteries and chargers, while others are sold as bare tools. Check exactly what is included before comparing prices.

Cordless Drill Buying Checklist

  • Do I need a drill-driver or a hammer drill?
  • Does the battery platform suit my future tool needs?
  • Is the drill comfortable to hold and control?
  • Does the kit include a battery and charger?
  • Are the warranty and return terms suitable?

Safety reminder: Follow the manufacturer's instructions, use appropriate protective equipment, and check for hidden wiring or plumbing before drilling.
